About

Clinician, engineer, builder

Trained in electrical engineering before medicine, and still working at that intersection.

I practice hematopathology at ARUP Laboratories, a national reference laboratory and nonprofit enterprise of the University of Utah and its Department of Pathology, where I direct hematologic flow cytometry and attend on the flow cytometry service. I also direct the Applied Artificial Intelligence group in the ARUP Institute for Research and Innovation.

My research asks how computation can make clinical flow cytometry faster, more reproducible, and more sensitive: automated case triage and diagnosis, self-supervised representation learning on large clinical datasets, real-time model deployment with monitoring, and high-parameter spectral panels for measurable residual disease (MRD).

I teach residents, fellows, technologists, and practicing pathologists through a lecture series on clinical flow cytometry, the flow cytometry rotation at ARUP, and courses for the International Clinical Cytometry Society (ICCS).

Research

Four threads of work

Each starts from a problem in the clinical laboratory and ends, where possible, in a validated tool that runs in production.

01

Machine learning for flow cytometry

Gate-free classification of B-cell malignancies using UMAP and random forests, comparative studies of published algorithms, and DinoFlow, a self-supervised transformer pretrained on 52,625 clinical samples to detect common hematologic disorders.

02

Deploying AI in the clinical laboratory

Cloud inference, reproducible workflows, structured data extraction from reports, and ongoing model monitoring, including a real-time AML detection system. Related work includes guidance on validation and performance metrics, and computer vision for acid-fast bacilli and Castleman disease histology.

03

MRD and high-parameter cytometry

Flow cytometric MRD in myeloma, CLL, and B-lymphoblastic leukemia; comparisons of flow with FISH; 25- to 37-marker spectral panels for lymphoid neoplasms and 27-color MRD for B-ALL; and study chair for SWOG S1925 (venetoclax and obinutuzumab in early high-risk CLL/SLL).

04

Quality, controls, and diagnostic stewardship

Synthetic cell particles as controls for rare populations such as mast cells, CD123 in systemic mastocytosis, patient-based quality control and delta checks for BCR-ABL1, and reflex myeloma FISH driven by flow MRD results to avoid uninformative testing.
